# T20 format
A T20 match has two innings of up to 20 overs each. An over is six legal deliveries. Wides and no balls are not legal deliveries, so the bowler must bowl them again. A team is all out when ten wickets fall. The team batting second chases a target of one run more than the first innings total.

# Run rate and required run rate
Current run rate is runs scored divided by overs faced, where 14.3 overs means 14 overs and 3 balls (14.5 in decimal). Required run rate is runs still needed divided by overs remaining, or 6 × runs needed ÷ balls left.

# Net run rate
Net run rate (NRR) ranks teams level on points. It is a team's runs scored per over across the tournament minus the runs conceded per over. A team bowled out is counted as having faced its full quota of overs.

# Wide ball
The umpire calls a wide when the ball passes too far from the batter to be hit with a normal cricket stroke. A wide adds one run plus any runs taken, does not count as a legal ball, and is not counted against the batter's balls faced. In limited-overs cricket wides are called strictly, including most deliveries passing down the leg side.

# No ball and free hit
A no ball is called most often when the bowler's front foot lands fully beyond the popping crease, or for a full toss above waist height. It adds one run to the batting side and is not a legal ball. In T20 cricket the next delivery after a front-foot no ball is a free hit: the batter can only be out in ways such as run out, not bowled, caught or lbw.

# Byes and leg byes
Byes are runs taken when the ball passes the batter without touching the bat or body. Leg byes are runs taken after the ball hits the batter's body while the batter was attempting a shot or avoiding the ball. Both count as extras and are not charged to the bowler.

# Ways of getting out
A batter can be out bowled, caught, leg before wicket (lbw), run out, stumped, hit wicket, obstructing the field, handled the ball (now part of obstructing the field), hit the ball twice, or timed out. Retired hurt is not a dismissal. The bowler is credited with bowled, caught, caught and bowled, lbw, stumped and hit wicket, but not run outs or obstructing the field.

# Leg before wicket (LBW)
A batter is out lbw when the ball would have gone on to hit the stumps but hit the batter's body first, subject to conditions. The ball must not pitch outside leg stump. If the batter plays a shot, the impact must be in line with the stumps. If the batter offers no shot, impact outside off stump can still be out.

# Decision Review System (DRS)
DRS lets a team challenge an on-field umpire's decision using ball tracking, edge detection (UltraEdge or Snicko) and replays. In the IPL each team gets a limited number of unsuccessful reviews per innings. For lbw, an "umpire's call" result means the on-field decision stands because the ball tracking was marginal, and the team keeps its review. Note: DRS was introduced to the IPL in 2018, after the seasons covered by this dataset.

# Powerplay and fielding restrictions
In a T20 innings the first six overs are the powerplay: only two fielders may be outside the 30-yard circle. From overs 7 to 20 up to five fielders may be outside the circle. Teams try to score fast in the powerplay while the field is up.

# Strategic timeout
The IPL has strategic timeouts of two and a half minutes per innings, one taken by the bowling side and one by the batting side within set over windows.

# Super over
If a T20 match is tied, a super over decides it. Each team bats one over with three batters (two wickets end it). The team with more runs wins. Super over deliveries are kept separate from the main match statistics.

# Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method
When rain shortens a match, the Duckworth-Lewis method (later Duckworth-Lewis-Stern, DLS) sets a revised target based on the resources (overs and wickets) each team had. In the dataset, dl_applied = 1 marks matches decided this way.

# Bowling limits
In a T20 match each bowler may bowl at most four overs. A bowler cannot bowl two consecutive overs.

# IPL format
The Indian Premier League (IPL) is a franchise T20 league in India that started in 2008. Teams play a league stage, then the top four reach the playoffs: Qualifier 1, the Eliminator, Qualifier 2, and the final. Points are two for a win and one for a no result.

# Strike rate and economy
A batter's strike rate is runs per 100 balls faced. A batter's average is runs divided by the number of times out. A bowler's economy rate is runs conceded per over. A bowler's average is runs conceded per wicket.

# Orange cap and purple cap
In the IPL the orange cap is worn by the season's leading run scorer and the purple cap by the leading wicket taker.

# About this chatbot's data
Historical statistics cover IPL seasons 2008 to 2017: 636 matches and about 150,000 deliveries. The live match is a ball-by-ball replay of a real match from this dataset. Player names use the scorecard format, for example MS Dhoni, V Kohli, RG Sharma, SK Raina, CH Gayle.
